## 📢 Why SID-Bench? (Real-World vs. Synthetic)

Most existing full-duplex benchmarks (e.g., FD-Bench, Full-Duplex-Bench) rely on **synthetic data** (text generated by LLMs and spoken by TTS). While scalable, synthetic speech fails to capture the intricate timing, prosody, hesitations, and emotional nuances of authentic human conversations.

**SID-Bench is the first benchmark for semantic-aware interruption detection built ENTIRELY from real-world human-to-human recorded dialogues.** 

### Core Features:
- **100% Real-World Audio**: Extracted from genuine, professionally recorded bilingual (English & Mandarin Chinese) conversations and the Switchboard corpus.
- **Intent-Aware Design**: Differentiates between **genuine interruptions** (user wants to take the floor) and **non-interruptive backchannels** (e.g., "uh-huh", "right", "I see").
- **Robustness Testing**: Includes 500 environmental noise and silence clips to stress-test models against non-semantic acoustic triggers.

## Evaluation Metrics

To resolve the speed-robustness trade-off, we adopt two primary metrics and propose one unified metric (**APT**). Reference implementation is in `test.ipynb`.

### 1. FIR — False Interruption Rate (Robustness)
The fraction of non-interruption samples where the model incorrectly predicts an interruption (lower is better).
$$FIR = \frac{|False\ Positives|}{|Total\ Nonbreak\ Samples|}$$

### 2. IRL — Interruption Response Latency (Speed)
The average absolute timing error (in seconds) over true-positive detections (lower is better).
$$IRL = \text{mean}(|T_{\text{model stop}} - T_{\text{break}}|) \quad \text{[over True Positives]}$$

### 3. APT — Average Penalty Time (The Composite Metric)
Proposed in our paper, **APT** assigns a "time cost" to the user for both false alarms and delayed responses:

| Outcome | Condition | Penalty Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **True Positive** | Correctly stops | $IRL$ (unavoidable latency) |
| **False Positive** | Catastrophic false alarm | $Duration$ of the planned turn (derails conversation) |
| **False Negative** | Fails to stop | $Duration - T_{\text{break}}$ (forces user to listen to unwanted speech) |
| **True Negative**| Correctly ignores backchannel | $0$ (ideal behavior) |

$$APT = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \text{PenaltyTime}_i \quad \text{(Lower is better)}$$

## Main Experimental Results

We evaluated our proposed **SID-model** (an Audio-Transformer + Qwen-0.6B architecture trained using a randomized temporal cropping paradigm) against mainstream full-duplex baselines.

| Model | EN APT ↓ | ZH APT ↓ | Noise/Silence APT ↓ | **Average APT ↓** | **Average FIR ↓** | **Average IRL (s) ↓** |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **FSMN-VAD** | 3.328 | 3.563 | 4.148 | **3.627** | 0.840 | **0.000** |
| **Freeze-Omni** (Silero VAD) | 2.429 | 1.977 | 1.908 | **2.129** | 0.532 | 0.173 |
| **FireRedChat** (pVAD) | 2.997 | 2.311 | 2.502 | **2.627** | 0.476 | 1.045 |
| **Moshi** (End-to-End) | 1.749 | - | 7.810 | **3.192** | 0.118 | 2.517 |
| **SID-model (Ours)** | **0.921** | **0.656** | **0.315** | **0.711** | **0.124** | 0.389 |

> **Key Finding**: Pure VAD-based systems are "trigger-happy" (high FIR), while fully end-to-end models like Moshi are too slow (high IRL). **Our proposed SID-model achieves a nearly 3-fold reduction in APT compared to the best baseline**, successfully balancing speed and robustness.
